The rulebook

The instruments that shape payments, crypto and AI in finance, with where each one stands today.

Region
Topic

37 instruments

  • The current EU rulebook for payment services: who needs a licence, strong customer authentication, and open access to payment accounts for licensed third parties. It stays in force until PSD3 and the PSR apply.

    Who it affects

    Banks, payment and e-money institutions, account information and payment initiation providers.

    Key dates

    1. 13 Jan 2018Applies across the EU

  • A directive on licensing, which folds e-money institutions into payment institutions, and a directly applicable regulation on conduct. Providers must refund customers tricked by someone impersonating their bank, payee verification extends to all credit transfers, and online platforms take on anti-fraud duties.

    Who it affects

    Every payment service provider in the EU, plus card schemes, technical service providers, very large platforms and telecoms.

    Key dates

    1. 27 Nov 2025Political agreement
    2. 29 Oct 2026Council adopts its first-reading position
    3. 14 Dec 2026Parliament second-reading vote (indicative)

    After publication: the PSR applies 21 months after entry into force, and existing licences must be re-assessed within 27 months.

  • One EU authorisation for crypto-asset service providers and a regime for stablecoins: e-money tokens and asset-referenced tokens. National grandfathering ended on 1 July 2026, so firms without an authorisation can no longer serve EU clients.

    Who it affects

    Exchanges, custodians, brokers and token issuers. Payments in e-money tokens can also trigger payments licensing.

    Key dates

    1. 30 Jun 2024Stablecoin rules apply
    2. 30 Dec 2024Service provider rules apply
    3. 1 Jul 2026Transitional period ends
    4. 30 Jun 2027Commission review report due

  • Originator and beneficiary information must travel with every crypto transfer, with no minimum amount. For transfers above EUR 1,000 to a self-hosted address, the provider must check that its customer controls the address.

    Who it affects

    Crypto-asset service providers and payment service providers.

    Key dates

    1. 30 Dec 2024Applies to crypto transfers

  • Technology risk management, incident reporting, resilience testing and mandatory contract terms with technology providers. Providers designated as critical are overseen at EU level.

    Who it affects

    Banks, payment and e-money institutions, crypto-asset service providers and, through their contracts, technology vendors.

    Key dates

    1. 17 Jan 2025Applies
    2. 18 Nov 2025First critical technology providers designated

  • Duties scale with risk. In finance, AI that scores the creditworthiness of individuals or prices life and health insurance is high-risk, and fraud detection is carved out. A 2026 amendment moved the high-risk deadline to 2 December 2027.

    Who it affects

    Providers and deployers of AI systems used in the EU, wherever they are established.

    Key dates

    1. 2 Feb 2025Prohibited practices
    2. 2 Aug 2025General-purpose model duties
    3. 2 Aug 2026General application, including transparency duties
    4. 2 Dec 2027High-risk systems

    Fines reach EUR 35 million or 7% of worldwide turnover for prohibited practices.

  • A single, directly applicable AML rulebook replaces national transpositions. Crypto firms and gambling providers are obliged entities, anonymous accounts and privacy coins are banned at regulated firms, and cash payments are capped at EUR 10,000. AMLA will directly supervise the highest-risk cross-border groups.

    Who it affects

    Financial institutions, crypto-asset service providers, gambling providers and other obliged entities.

    Key dates

    1. 1 Jul 2025AMLA becomes operational
    2. 10 Jul 2027Regulation applies
    3. 2028Direct supervision by AMLA begins

  • Euro credit transfers in ten seconds, around the clock, at no higher price than a standard transfer, with the payee's name verified before the payer confirms.

    Who it affects

    Banks first. Payment and e-money institutions in the euro area must send and receive from 9 April 2027.

    Key dates

    1. 9 Jan 2025Euro-area banks must receive
    2. 9 Oct 2025Euro-area banks must send, with payee verification
    3. 9 Apr 2027Euro-area payment and e-money institutions
    4. 9 Jul 2027Non-euro Member States: sending and verification

  • Brings most buy-now-pay-later into consumer credit law. Third-party BNPL needs a creditworthiness assessment and full pre-contract information. Only a merchant's own short, free deferral stays outside.

    Who it affects

    Lenders, BNPL providers and the merchants that distribute credit.

    Key dates

    1. 20 Nov 2026Applies in the Member States

  • Every Member State must offer a digital identity wallet. Banks and payment providers that are required to use strong authentication will have to accept it when the customer asks.

    Who it affects

    Banks, payment providers and other services that rely on strong user authentication.

    Key dates

    1. 24 Dec 2026Member States offer wallets
    2. 24 Dec 2027Acceptance duty for regulated services

  • Article 22 limits decisions based solely on automated processing. The Court of Justice held in SCHUFA that a credit score can itself be such a decision when a lender draws strongly on it, and in 2025 confirmed a right to an explanation of the logic actually applied.

    Who it affects

    Anyone processing personal data of people in the EU, including from outside it.

    Key dates

    1. 7 Dec 2023SCHUFA judgment (C-634/21)
    2. 27 Feb 2025Judgment on the right to an explanation (C-203/22)

  • Crypto-asset service providers report their users' transactions to tax authorities, which exchange the data across the EU. 2026 is the first reporting year.

    Who it affects

    Crypto-asset service providers with EU users.

    Key dates

    1. 1 Jan 2026Applies

  • Consumer banking, payment services, payment terminals and e-commerce must be accessible. Existing contracts and equipment have until 28 June 2030.

    Who it affects

    Banks, payment providers and online merchants serving consumers in the EU.

    Key dates

    1. 28 Jun 2025Applies

  • Would extend open banking to open finance: loans, savings, investments, pensions and insurance. Still in negotiation, with a narrower, phased scope under discussion.

    Who it affects

    Financial institutions that hold customer data, and the firms that want to use it.

  • A retail central bank currency for the euro area. The legislation is in three-way negotiation. The ECB plans a pilot from the second half of 2027 and a possible first issuance in 2029.

    Who it affects

    Banks and payment providers, which would distribute it.

    Key dates

    1. 9 Jul 2026Parliament adopts its negotiating position

  • The contract layer. It governs the relationship between a payment service provider and its customer: written terms and disclosure, capped customer liability for misuse, refunds, and when a payment instrument may be blocked. It cannot be waived to the customer's detriment.

    Who it affects

    Issuers, acquirers and payment account providers serving customers in Israel.

    Key dates

    1. 14 Oct 2020In force
    2. 17 May 2026Memorandum on sharing fraud data between providers

  • The licence layer. The Israel Securities Authority licenses payment companies for payment accounts, issuing, acquiring and initiation. A foreign licence carries no passport, although the Authority may license a foreign provider and relax some conditions. Since the transition ended on 6 June 2026, these services cannot be provided without a licence.

    Who it affects

    Non-bank payment providers active in Israel. Banks and the card companies are supervised by the Bank of Israel.

    Key dates

    1. 6 Jun 2024In force
    2. 6 Jun 2026Transition period ends
    3. 6 Dec 2026Payment companies open accounts to advanced initiation

    Initial capital ranges from NIS 80,000 to NIS 1.4 million, depending on the service.

  • Israel's open banking law. With the customer's consent, licensed providers can read account data from banks, card companies, lenders and, since June 2026, payment companies.

    Who it affects

    Information service providers licensed by the Securities Authority, and the institutions that must share data.

    Key dates

    1. 14 Jun 2022In force
    2. 1 Jun 2026Payment companies become data sources

  • Capital Market Authority licences for credit providers, peer-to-peer lending platforms and services in financial assets, which include virtual currency. New capital requirements for crypto licensees were set in July 2026.

    Who it affects

    Lenders, currency service providers and crypto businesses serving Israel.

    Key dates

    1. 27 Apr 2026First shekel stablecoin approved, in a limited scope
    2. 19 Jul 2026Capital circular for virtual currency licensees

  • Sector orders set identification, reporting and record-keeping duties. One order covers crypto and credit providers and another covers payment companies. The travel rule applies to virtual currency transfers.

    Who it affects

    Banks, payment companies, credit providers and crypto service providers.

    Key dates

    1. Nov 2021Order for crypto and credit providers in force
    2. 10 Jun 2026Securities Authority draft AML directive for payment companies

  • The Supervisor of Banks' AML directive. An October 2026 amendment bars a bank from refusing payment services merely because the funds originate in virtual currency. The directive also restricts card charges for prohibited gambling and treats gambling as a high-risk sector for acquiring.

    Who it affects

    Banks and card companies, and through them every crypto business and gaming merchant that needs an Israeli account or acquirer.

    Key dates

    1. 7 Oct 2026Amendment published
    2. 1 May 2027Amendment in force

  • The largest reform of Israeli privacy law in decades: updated definitions of personal and especially sensitive information, a privacy protection officer for certain organisations, and administrative fines that grow with the size of the database.

    Who it affects

    Any organisation holding personal data in Israel. Banks, insurers and large processors of sensitive data must appoint an officer.

    Key dates

    1. 14 Aug 2025In force

  • Israel has no horizontal AI statute. Policy is sectoral and risk-based. The inter-ministerial report on AI in finance recommends focusing on high-risk systems, telling customers when AI is used, and keeping responsibility with the supervised firm.

    Who it affects

    Supervised financial entities that build or buy AI systems.

    Key dates

    1. Dec 2023National AI regulation and ethics policy
    2. 24 Dec 2025Final report on AI in the financial sector

  • Two reforms in consultation. One would license stablecoin issuers under the Capital Market Authority, with fully segregated reserves and redemption at par. The other would bring digital investment assets and trading venues under securities law.

    Who it affects

    Stablecoin issuers, crypto trading venues and advisers serving Israel.

    Key dates

    1. 29 Jun 2026Stablecoin memorandum published
    2. 2 Nov 2026Digital assets memorandum published
    3. 22 Nov 2026Comment period closes

  • The UK's own version of PSD2. Since 7 May 2026 the FCA requires tighter reconciliations, a monthly return and an annual safeguarding audit.

    Who it affects

    Payment and e-money institutions authorised or registered in the UK.

    Key dates

    1. 7 May 2026New safeguarding regime in force

  • Payment firms must reimburse victims of authorised push payment fraud over Faster Payments and CHAPS, up to GBP 85,000 per claim, with the cost shared between the sending and the receiving firm.

    Who it affects

    Banks and payment firms that send or receive over the UK payment systems.

    Key dates

    1. 7 Oct 2024Mandatory reimbursement begins

  • Trading platforms, dealing, custody, staking and stablecoin issuance become regulated activities under the Financial Services and Markets Act. Firms must apply through the FCA gateway, and approval is not automatic.

    Who it affects

    Crypto firms serving UK customers, including firms currently registered only for AML purposes.

    Key dates

    1. 30 Jun 2026Final rules published
    2. 30 Sep 2026Application gateway opens
    3. 28 Feb 2027Application gateway closes
    4. 25 Oct 2027Regime starts

  • The first federal framework for payment stablecoins: permitted issuers only, one-to-one reserves in cash and short-dated Treasuries, redemption rights and bank-style AML duties. A foreign issuer needs a comparable home regime to reach US users.

    Who it affects

    Stablecoin issuers, and the exchanges and wallets that offer their tokens in the US.

    Key dates

    1. 18 Jul 2025Signed into law
    2. 21 Oct 2026OCC final rule
    3. 18 Jan 2027Effective date

  • There is no single US payments licence. Moving money for others generally needs a licence in each state where the customers are, plus federal registration as a money services business.

    Who it affects

    Payment companies, wallets and crypto businesses serving US customers. Many launch through a licensed partner instead.

  • The 2024 rule giving consumers the right to share their financial data has been stayed by a court since October 2025 while the CFPB rewrites it. Until then, data access runs on private agreements.

    Who it affects

    Banks, card issuers, data aggregators and the fintechs that depend on account data.

  • Widens the licensing perimeter to technology providers and platforms that facilitate licensed financial activities, with fines of up to AED 1 billion. The one-year transition ran to September 2026.

    Who it affects

    Payment, wallet and crypto payment businesses serving the UAE onshore, and their technology partners.

  • Activity-based licences for virtual asset businesses in Dubai outside the DIFC, with separate rulebooks for exchange, custody, broker-dealer, advisory and issuance.

    Who it affects

    Crypto businesses operating in or from Dubai.

    Key dates

    1. 19 Jun 2025Rulebooks version 2.0 applies

  • The global standard behind every travel rule. The June 2025 revision standardises the originator and beneficiary information that must accompany cross-border payments. Countries have until the end of 2030 to implement it.

    Who it affects

    Banks, payment providers and crypto firms that send value across borders.

    Key dates

    1. 18 Jun 2025Revised recommendation adopted

  • The card industry's security standard for anyone that stores, processes or transmits card data. Version 4.0.1 is the only active version, and its future-dated requirements became mandatory on 31 March 2025.

    Who it affects

    Merchants, gateways, processors and their service providers.

    Key dates

    1. 31 Mar 2025All v4 requirements mandatory

  • One ratio that combines fraud and disputes, measured for acquirers and for merchants. The merchant threshold for excessive status tightened in April 2026 in the US, Canada, the EU and Asia-Pacific, which matters most to high-volume online merchants.

    Who it affects

    Acquirers and, through their contracts, every merchant that accepts Visa.

    Key dates

    1. 1 Apr 2025Programme launches
    2. 1 Oct 2025Enforcement begins
    3. 1 Apr 2026Merchant threshold tightens

    Scheme rules change often. Check thresholds against the current programme guide.

  • The structured message standard for payments. Swift ended the coexistence period for cross-border payment instructions on 22 November 2025. The removal of unstructured addresses, planned for November 2026, was postponed.

    Who it affects

    Banks and payment providers that send or receive cross-border payments.

    Key dates

    1. 22 Nov 2025Coexistence period ends

  • Industry protocols that let AI agents pay: Google's AP2, now with the FIDO Alliance, the Agentic Commerce Protocol from OpenAI and Stripe, Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol, Mastercard Agent Pay and x402. None is law, and no regulator has issued agent-specific payment rules, so the existing rules on consent, authentication and liability apply.

    Who it affects

    Merchants, payment providers and AI companies building checkout into agents.

    Key dates

    1. 29 Sep 2025Agentic Commerce Protocol launches
    2. 28 Apr 2026AP2 donated to the FIDO Alliance

One payment, seven rulebooks

Follow a single transaction from the tap to settlement and see which rules apply at each step.

Initiation Merchant Gateway and PSP Acquirer Network Issuer or account bank Settlement
  1. 1

    A customer, or their AI agent, decides to pay

    Initiation

    Before any money moves, the law asks who agreed to what: consent, authentication and, for an agent, the scope of its mandate.

    • Consent and mandate
    • Strong customer authentication
    • Consumer disclosure
  2. 2

    The checkout takes the payment

    Merchant

    How the merchant takes the payment decides whether it touches card data, and whether a marketplace is itself a regulated payment provider.

    • PCI DSS scope
    • Surcharging and pricing rules
    • Marketplace exemptions
  3. 3

    A provider routes the transaction

    Gateway and PSP

    The moment a provider controls customer funds it needs a licence or a licensed partner, and those funds must be safeguarded.

    • Licence perimeter
    • Safeguarding
    • Outsourcing and DORA
  4. 4

    The acquirer answers for its merchants

    Acquirer

    Underwriting, category codes and fraud ratios decide which merchants stay connected.

    • Merchant underwriting
    • Scheme monitoring programmes
    • Correct category codes
  5. 5

    The scheme or payment system carries the message

    Network

    Card schemes and payment systems add private rulebooks on top of public law, and they change faster than statutes do.

    • Scheme rules
    • Interchange caps
    • Tokenisation and agent programmes
  6. 6

    The payer's provider approves or declines

    Issuer or account bank

    It authenticates, screens and, when something goes wrong, usually refunds first and recovers later.

    • Fraud liability and refunds
    • Verification of payee
    • Sanctions and AML screening
  7. 7

    Funds move between institutions

    Settlement

    Timing, currency and the settlement asset each carry their own rules, including when the asset is a stablecoin.

    • Settlement finality
    • Currency conversion and reporting
    • Stablecoin settlement
