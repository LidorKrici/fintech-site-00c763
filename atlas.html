Regulatory atlas

Where your customers are decides which rulebook applies. Turn the globe or pick a jurisdiction.

Israel

Regulators

Israel Securities Authority, Bank of Israel, Capital Market Authority, Privacy Protection Authority

Key frameworks

  • Payment Services Law, 2019The contract between provider and customer: disclosure, capped liability for misuse, refunds.
  • Payment Services Licensing Law, 2023A Securities Authority licence for issuing, acquiring, payment accounts and initiation. The transition period ended on 6 June 2026.
  • Financial Information Service Law, 2021Open banking. Payment companies became data sources on 1 June 2026.
  • Supervision of Financial Services Law, 2016Capital Market Authority licences for credit and for services in financial assets, including virtual currency.
  • Privacy Protection Law, Amendment 13In force since 14 August 2025: new definitions, a privacy protection officer for certain bodies, monetary sanctions.

WatchTwo memoranda are open: stablecoin issuance (June 2026) and securities law for digital assets (comments until 22 November 2026).

European Union

Regulators

National competent authorities, with the EBA and ESMA at EU level and AMLA supervising directly from 2028

Key frameworks

  • PSD2, then PSD3 and the PSRLicensing and conduct for payment and e-money institutions. One licence passports across the EEA.
  • MiCAA single authorisation for crypto-asset service providers and the rules for stablecoin issuers.
  • AI ActRisk-based duties for AI systems. Credit scoring and life and health insurance pricing are high-risk.
  • AML Regulation and AMLAA single AML rulebook from 10 July 2027 and a new EU supervisor in Frankfurt.
  • DORATechnology risk, incident reporting and third-party contracts for financial entities, since January 2025.

WatchPSD3 and the PSR await the Parliament's second-reading vote, pencilled in for 14 December 2026. The PSR would apply 21 months after it enters into force.

United Kingdom

Regulators

Financial Conduct Authority, Bank of England, and the Payment Systems Regulator, which is being folded into the FCA

Key frameworks

  • Payment Services and E-Money RegulationsAuthorisation for payment and e-money institutions. Tighter safeguarding rules apply since 7 May 2026.
  • APP fraud reimbursementMandatory refunds for authorised push payment fraud since 7 October 2024, up to GBP 85,000 per claim.
  • Cryptoasset regimeFull FCA authorisation replaces AML registration. Applications are open until 28 February 2027 and the regime starts on 25 October 2027.
  • Buy-now-pay-laterThird-party interest-free credit is regulated since 15 July 2026.
  • AINo AI statute. The FCA applies its existing rules and has said it does not plan AI-specific ones.

WatchThe Treasury consulted in 2026 on rewriting payment services law. The outcome is pending.

United States

Regulators

State regulators, FinCEN, the OCC, the Federal Reserve and the CFPB

Key frameworks

  • State money transmitter licencesLicensing state by state. 31 states have adopted the model Money Transmission Modernization Act in full or in part.
  • FinCEN registrationFederal registration as a money services business, with a Bank Secrecy Act compliance programme.
  • GENIUS ActThe federal framework for payment stablecoin issuers. Effective 18 January 2027. The OCC published its final rule on 21 October 2026.
  • AINo federal AI statute. State laws fill the gap, and fair-lending rules require specific reasons for any model-driven credit denial.

WatchThe crypto market structure bill failed a Senate vote on 15 September 2026. It lapses in January 2027 unless Congress passes it first.

United Arab Emirates

Regulators

Central Bank of the UAE, VARA in Dubai, the FSRA in ADGM and the DFSA in DIFC

Key frameworks

  • Central bank law of 2025A wider licensing perimeter that reaches technology providers supporting licensed financial activities. The one-year transition ended in September 2026.
  • Retail Payment Services and Card Schemes RegulationThe licence categories for payment services onshore.
  • Payment Token Services RegulationOnshore payments in stablecoins are limited to licensed dirham payment tokens.
  • VARA rulebooksDubai's virtual asset licences. Version 2.0 of the rulebooks has applied since June 2025.

WatchThe free zones (ADGM, DIFC) run their own regimes. Check which regulator covers your model before you choose an address.

Malta

Regulators

Malta Financial Services Authority, Malta Gaming Authority

Key frameworks

  • EU passportPayment, e-money and MiCA authorisations granted in Malta passport across the EEA.
  • MiCA transitionThe national transition from the earlier virtual financial assets regime ended on 30 June 2026.
  • GamingMGA licences are a common base for operators, which makes payment flows a central part of any application.

WatchThe EU is negotiating whether ESMA should supervise significant crypto firms directly.

Cyprus

Regulators

Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission, Central Bank of Cyprus

Key frameworks

  • Payments and e-moneyThe Central Bank authorises payment and e-money institutions, with an EEA passport.
  • MiCACySEC is the competent authority for crypto-asset service providers. The transition ended on 1 July 2026.
  • Investment firmsA long-standing base for CFD and forex brokers, with matching scrutiny of their payment flows.

Singapore

Regulators

Monetary Authority of Singapore

Key frameworks

  • Payment Services ActOne licensing framework for payments, e-money and digital payment token services.
  • Overseas-facing token firmsSince 30 June 2025, digital token service providers that serve only overseas customers from Singapore also need a licence.
  • StablecoinsMAS consulted in autumn 2026 on legislation for a stablecoin issuer licence.

Hong Kong

Regulators

Hong Kong Monetary Authority, Securities and Futures Commission

Key frameworks

  • Stablecoins OrdinanceIssuing fiat-referenced stablecoins has needed an HKMA licence since 1 August 2025. The first licences were granted in April 2026.
  • Stored value facilitiesHKMA licences for e-wallets and prepaid products.
  • Trading platformsSFC licensing for virtual asset trading platforms.

Brazil

Regulators

Banco Central do Brasil, and the Secretariat of Prizes and Bets at the Ministry of Finance

Key frameworks

  • PixThe central bank's instant payment system, now the default way to pay in Brazil.
  • Payment institutionsCentral bank authorisation for payment institutions and for participation in Pix.
  • Virtual assetsCentral bank rules for virtual asset service providers took effect in February 2026.

WatchProvisional Measure 1.394 of 25 September 2026 prohibited fixed-odds betting nationwide and blocked deposits to betting platforms. Congress must convert it into law or it lapses.
